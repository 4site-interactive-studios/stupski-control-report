🚨
The promo modal remains the #1 UX issue across both device categories: On mobile, 1,781 taps (35.6% of 5,000 total taps) went to closing the promo modal vs. just 33 taps (0.7%) on the modal's CTA button, a 54:1 ratio of dismissals to engagements. On desktop, 454 clicks (19.5% of 2,323 total clicks) went to the modal close vs. 24 clicks (1.0%) on its CTA, a 19:1 ratio. The pattern was consistent across the period and across the Time100 traffic surge, which tells us the issue isn't audience-specific. It's the modal itself.

What this means: The modal is interrupting the visit before users have a chance to engage with the page they came to see. Visitors who arrived from LinkedIn shares, Facebook ads, branded search, or Time100 press coverage all hit the same interruption, and the overwhelming majority dismiss it. The cost isn't just the wasted modal impression, it's the friction added to the rest of the visit, which likely contributes to the 90.5% scroll drop-off at 10%. See the Recommendations tab for prioritized fixes.
⚠️
iOS JavaScript errors intensified across the expanded period: 598 sessions (43.87% of all JS errors) encountered the "window.webkit.messageHandlers" error, up from 465 sessions (41%) at May 1. This error pattern is commonly observed in iOS webview contexts, including in-app browsers like the Facebook app's webview. Total JavaScript errors across the site reached 1,363 sessions in the 92-day period, and the share attributable to this single iOS error continues to grow.

What this means: Something on the page is calling a JavaScript bridge that exists in some webview contexts but not all. When the bridge isn't available (Safari proper, Chrome on iOS, regular iOS browsing), the call fails. The error itself usually doesn't crash the page, but it can prevent specific functionality from working, things like video playback, form submission, or modal interactions. Given mobile's dominant share of /control/ traffic at 74.2%, this issue is impacting close to half of mobile visitors. See the Recommendations tab for the dev fix.

Performance & Behavior Signals

Clarity

Core Web Vitals and behavioral insights

Core Web Vitals
Behavioral Insights
💡
What Core Web Vitals are telling us: Core Web Vitals are Google's standardized measurements of how a page actually performs for real users, focused on three things: how fast it loads, how responsive it is to interaction, and how stable the visual layout is. The CONTROL page's scores show meaningful room for improvement across all three.

• Performance Score: 57.2. Anything below 70 is "needs improvement," and pages above 90 are "good." A 57.2 means the page is functional but slower or less stable than what most visitors are used to elsewhere.
• LCP (load time): 3.587s. Measures how long it takes for the largest visible element (usually the hero image or video poster) to render. Google's threshold for "good" is under 2.5 seconds. At 3.6 seconds, visitors are waiting roughly 40% longer than they should.
• INP (interaction responsiveness): 368ms. Measures how quickly the page reacts when someone taps or clicks. Google's threshold for "good" is under 200ms. At 368ms, visitors perceive a noticeable lag between their tap and the page responding.
• CLS (visual stability): 0.498. Measures how much content shifts as the page loads. Google's threshold for "good" is under 0.1. At 0.498, the page is shifting significantly, often because images, ads, or embeds load after the initial render and push other elements around.

What this means: Visitors arriving from any source are getting a page that loads slowly, responds slowly, and shifts around as they're trying to interact with it. That combination compounds the modal friction: by the time the page settles, the modal has already interrupted them, and the page below feels sluggish when they try to engage. It's a meaningful headwind on every other engagement metric in the dashboard. See the Recommendations tab for the prioritized fix sequence. Thresholds sourced from Google's web.dev Core Web Vitals guidance.
💡
What the Behavioral Insights are telling us: Clarity tracks four behavioral signals that supplement the engagement metrics from GA4. Each measures a different kind of "user is frustrated" moment.

• Dead Clicks: 656 sessions (1.66%). A tap or click on something that looks clickable but isn't. Suggests visitors are encountering elements they expect to be interactive but aren't.
• Quick Back Clicks: 345 sessions (0.87%). A user lands and almost immediately hits the browser back button. Modern version of a "bounce with intent to escape." The timing and pattern are consistent with what we'd expect from low-quality paid traffic.
• Rage Clicks: 11 sessions (0.03%). Repeated clicking on the same element in frustration. The number is low, which is genuinely good. Visitors aren't encountering broken or unresponsive elements often enough to provoke that pattern.
• Excessive Scrolling: 0 sessions (0%). Flags users who scroll up and down repeatedly trying to find something. The fact that this is zero, combined with the 90% scroll drop-off at 10%, tells a clear story: visitors aren't getting lost or hunting for content, they're just leaving before they ever scroll meaningfully.

What this means: The behavioral signals reinforce what the click and scroll data already show. The CONTROL page works well enough mechanically (low rage clicks, no excessive scrolling). What it doesn't do is give visitors a compelling reason to stay engaged past the modal interruption and the initial load. Fixing the modal trigger and the Core Web Vitals issues should reduce dead clicks and quick back clicks as a downstream effect.

Recommendations for Increasing Engagement and Conversion

Specific actions for driving CONTROL book purchases and improving site engagement, organized by effort

📌
This section translates the findings in the dashboard into specific actions. Recommendations are grouped by effort, what can ship this week with no design or dev required, what requires a design or dev cycle, and longer-term content and SEO investments. A separate Strategic Considerations document accompanies this dashboard for forward-looking planning decisions.
⚡

Quick Wins

Ship this week, no design or dev required

Remove the promo modal on mobile
Highest-impact single change available. Mobile is 74.2% of /control/ traffic, and the modal close-to-CTA ratio there is 54:1. One configuration change in the modal's display logic.
Add UTM tagging to LinkedIn shares from Stupski staff and partners
Recovers the LinkedIn attribution currently bucketed as Direct due to LinkedIn's redirect behavior. We recommend building a small library of pre-tagged URLs in a shared Drive doc for common campaigns.
Update the /break-fake-rules/ page title and meta description
Disambiguate the page as a podcast page. Updating the title to "Break Fake Rules Podcast | Glen Galaich | Stupski Foundation" signals podcast context immediately in search results and should reduce the misattributed impression problem.
Update the Time100 banner to include the /control/ landing page
Currently the Time100 banner runs site-wide except on /control/. Visitors arriving from the Time100 article deserve a clear next step toward the book, especially when they land on the book's own page.
🔧

Conversion Optimization

Requires design or dev cycles, ships within 30 days

Switch the desktop modal trigger from immediate to delayed
Currently the modal appears on page load. Change it to fire on either scroll past 25% or 15 seconds of dwell time, whichever comes first. Visitors clearly bouncing won't see it, and visitors engaged enough to scroll or linger have signaled interest. Exit-intent (mouse moving toward the browser close button) is worth testing as an alternative.
Fix CLS by adding explicit dimensions to above-the-fold media
Single development session, immediate impact on Core Web Vitals and perceived stability. Adding width and height attributes to the hero image and other above-the-fold media stabilizes layout as the page loads.
Optimize the hero image and video poster for faster LCP
Compress the image, serve it in a modern format like WebP or AVIF, and lazy-load anything below the fold. Should bring LCP from 3.6s into the "good" range under 2.5s.
Audit the JavaScript bundle for INP improvements
A 368ms INP usually means JavaScript is running heavily on interaction. Common causes to investigate include large analytics or tag manager configurations, third-party widgets, and scripts that block the main thread. Scope to be confirmed by the dev team after initial review.
Add a sticky "Buy the Book" CTA bar on mobile
Appears after 25% scroll. Mobile visitors who engage past the modal currently have to scroll back up to find purchase links. A persistent CTA captures that intent at the moment it's strongest.
Fix the iOS webkit messageHandlers error
The dev team should identify the script calling window.webkit.messageHandlers and add a defensive check before the call: if (window.webkit && window.webkit.messageHandlers) { ... }. This is a standard iOS webview defensive coding pattern. The exact scope depends on which script is responsible.
Test a simplified hero with a single primary CTA
The current hero has multiple CTAs competing for attention. A/B test against a single "Buy CONTROL" primary CTA with a secondary "Learn more" anchor below.
📚

Content & SEO

Ships within 60 to 90 days

Build a dedicated /press/ or /in-the-news/ page
Consolidate Time100 and other external mentions in one place. Captures branded search intent currently landing on the homepage and gives Google more context for future zero-click answers.
Add PodcastSeries schema markup to /break-fake-rules/
Helps Google understand the page as a podcast resource rather than generic content. Works alongside the title and meta description fixes from Quick Wins. Schema sourced from the schema.org PodcastSeries specification.
Internal linking audit and refresh
Add intentional links from /control/ to /people/glen-galaich-ph-d/ and to /break-fake-rules/, and from those pages back to /control/. Currently these pages live in relative isolation from each other, which limits how Google understands their relationship and how visitors discover the full ecosystem.
Optimize for the emerging AI Assistant channel
4 sessions is small, but it's the first signal that LLMs are citing Stupski content directly. Building clear, well-structured FAQ and About content on /control/ would improve discoverability in AI-generated answers, where citations matter more than traditional rankings.
Monitor the Break Fake Rules anomaly after fixes
If the misattributed impressions don't drop within 60 days of the Quick Wins updates, the next step is submitting a URL-level signal to Google Search Console asking it to re-evaluate the page intent. 4Site can handle this as part of regular site maintenance.
